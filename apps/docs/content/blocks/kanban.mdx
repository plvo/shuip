`Kanban` is a generic board (`Kanban<T>`) that renders any array of typed items into draggable columns. Columns, the property fields shown on each card, and the searchable fields are all configured against your own data shape — nothing is hardcoded to a domain.

### Built-in features

- **Generic data model**: works with any item type `T` via `idField` / `columnField`.
- **Structured cards**: the title is rendered from `title(item)` (as a `CardTitle`, so it can be a string, a link, or any element); the content area is driven by a `fields` config or fully customized with `cardContent`.
- **Inter and intra-column drag-and-drop**: move cards between columns and reorder within a column. A drop slot of the card's height shows where it will land.
- **Hybrid state**: works standalone from `defaultData`, or controlled via `data` + `onDataChange`.
- **Global search**: filter cards across `searchableFields`.
- **Per-column extras**: count, a `renderColumnSummary` slot, color accent, and an add button.
- **Ordered cards**: name a numeric field in `positionField` and each column keeps its cards sorted by it.

> Without `positionField`, the card order is implicit in the `data` array order. On any move, the board emits the reordered array via `onDataChange` (and a semantic `onCardMove` event); map that to your own persistence. To store the order of cards as a number instead, see [Keeping the order of cards](#keeping-the-order-of-cards).

> Define your item type with a `type` alias, not an `interface` — `Kanban<T>` requires `T extends Record<string, unknown>`, which a TypeScript `interface` does not satisfy.

> For boards that outgrow one screen — hundreds of cards, grouping into bands, hideable columns, a
> persisted view — see [Data Board](/blocks/data-board).

## Keeping the order of cards

Name a numeric field of your items in `positionField` and the board sorts every column by it, in
ascending order. Cards of equal position keep their order from `data`, and a value that is not a
finite number (missing, `null`, `NaN`) counts as `0`.

```tsx
<Kanban<Task> positionField='position' {...props} />
```

On drop, the board writes a new position into the moved card. It is computed from the neighbours of
the landing slot in the **full** order of the target column: cards hidden by the search count as
neighbours, so a drop never jumps over a card the user cannot see, and the dragged card does not
count.

| Landing slot | New position |
| --- | --- |
| Empty column | `0` |
| First | `next - 1` |
| Last | `previous + 1` |
| Between two cards | midpoint of their positions |

The moved card comes back from `onDataChange` with the field overwritten, and `onCardMove` carries the
same value as `position`, so persisting a move is one update of one record:

```tsx
<Kanban<Task>
  columnField='status'
  positionField='position'
  onCardMove={({ item, toColumn, position }) => updateTask(item.id, { status: toColumn, position })}
  {...props}
/>
```

`onDataChange` receives the full array: every card, sorted by position (equal positions keep their
previous order), with the moved card carrying its new position. Pass it back through `data` and the
board shows exactly that.

The neighbours of the moved card are named on every move event, with or without `positionField`.
`beforeId` and `afterId` are the ids of the cards right before and right after it in the full order of
its new column, and `null` at the edges. Use them to store the order as a linked list, or to call an
API that takes an `afterId`.

Each move into the same gap halves it, and a float64 holds about 50 halvings. After roughly 50 moves
into one gap, the midpoint equals the position of a neighbour, and the order of those two cards is then
only the order of `data`. The board does not renumber positions for you. When the `position` of a move
equals the position of its `beforeId` or `afterId` card, renumber that column on your side, for example
to `1..n` or with a wide step such as `1000`, and save the new values.

## Notes and limitations

- **`toIndex` is the index among the whole column.** Cards hidden by the search count, and with
  `positionField` the index is counted in the position-sorted list.
- **Cards move on drop, not on hover.** While a card is held, the others stay where they are and a
  drop slot of the card's height shows where it would land. The move is applied once, when the card
  is released: right after the card it was held below, right before the card it was held above, or
  right after the last card shown when it is released on the column itself, so it never jumps over a
  card hidden by the search. Releasing a card outside any column, on its own slot, or pressing
  Escape leaves the board as it was and fires neither `onCardMove` nor `onDataChange`.
- **A `data` update received mid-drag is kept.** The board applies it once the drag ends; a move
  dropped meanwhile is replayed between the same neighbours in the new list.
